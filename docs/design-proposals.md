# Design proposals

Decisions made beyond the Livepeer brand anchors, kept as a running list so they can be proposed back as an
update to Livepeer's design guidelines. Anchors themselves (semantic tokens, green only for non-interactive
brand expression, Inter + Geist Mono, mono logo, 4px rhythm) are unchanged. Screenshots are in
`docs/screenshots/`; they were taken in mock-only mode at 1440×900 and 375×812.

| 22 | **Footage behind the home composer.** The instance's own looping video (`home.backgroundVideo` in the config) fills the top of Home under a scrim, fading into the panel before the gallery; the composer and the starting-point chips become liquid glass over it (the `glass` utility: a see-through fill, 24px blur with extra saturation, a bright edge and a top highlight, tuned per theme). Two looks: `LivingStill` drifts the frame and lets the photo's coloured light ripple, for any photo; `AuroraSky` (`home.aurora: true`) keeps the photo's sky, stars and horizon, takes out its coloured glow and draws aurora curtains over it in code: thin sheets of light folding between two altitudes, seen from the ground and coloured by height. Stars twinkle in both, each on its own rhythm. In the light theme `AuroraSky` drops the night sky and lets the same aurora tint a pale sky, like watercolour on paper (the shader reads a `paper` uniform), under a light 15% veil; the night photo is never shown there. The `full` preset ships a generated night-sky photograph (`/home/aurora.webp`, 90 KB) with the code aurora. With neither image nor video, soft drifting light stands in. Tried and dropped along the way: generated clips (slowed, they were choppy and grainy), a fully code-drawn sky (never looked natural), and an animated photo alone (natural, but the motion was a ripple rather than an aurora); the photo sky with code curtains won a side-by-side comparison (`docs/screenshots/aurora-lab-comparison.jpg`). | 23 | **The agent's model on Home and Chat; capabilities in the studio.** Following the reference's agentic chat, the composer's menu picks the language model that runs the agent (Auto, then the models on the network, each with a line on what sets it apart and Busy when no runner is free), never a capability. What gets made is the agent's call, steered by the words, what is attached, a cast character and the starting points, and shown in its steps. The chosen model carries on through the conversation. A plain app such as `chat` is named by the model its runners report. The menu lists models, not deployments: apps serving the same model share one row that sends to the copy with a runner free and the most context. Who runs a model is never shown. With fewer than two models the menu is hidden, since Auto is the only real choice. The list comes from `listAgentModels`, mock while the agent is mocked and switched to live with it, never mixing invented and real models in one list. The models that do the work appear where the work is chosen or shown: under each capability in the studio picker ("Text to image · FLUX.2 klein 4B") and on each of the agent's steps. A narrow model under a broad capability is named for what it does (BiRefNet under `image-edit` reads "Remove background"). Capabilities are picked directly in the studio, from those that fit the medium, as in the reference's classic chat. Discovery mixes the two kinds in app names (`blueclaw/chat/qwen3.8-27b` is chat served by Qwen3.8 27B), so `lib/capability-menu.ts` sorts them: names with a model become models, the rest are capabilities, and `appName` names each by what it is. Menus over the page use `glass-menu` with `bg-glass-menu` and open upward from the composer. | One kind of thing per list, so models and capabilities never sit side by side. | `AgentModelPicker`, `OfferingPicker`, `lib/capability-menu.ts` | — |
| 24 | **Surfaces follow what the instance serves, and the console can't switch itself off.** Settings → Surfaces switches the user app, the API and the MCP server; the console is shown as a switch that is on and disabled, because switching it off from inside locks every operator out with no way back but the config file. A save that switches a surface off opens a red review sheet first, naming what stops for whom ("Keep it on" / "Turn off and save"); saves that only switch things on, or change other settings, go straight through. Running without it is a deployment choice (`surfaces.console` in the config), for example a public app-only instance beside an internal console-only one. The Developers section documents the API and the MCP server, so it follows them: with both off it is hidden from the sidebar and its pages return 404; with the API off the playground and examples go and the overview and model pages drop their API code; with MCP off the MCP setup goes. The library's "Convert or export" tools open model pages, so they are hidden with the section. | A switch that only does harm isn't offered, and users aren't shown keys or docs for something they can't use. | `SettingsView`, `lib/developers.ts`, `proxy.ts` | — |
| 25 | **Plan and credits carries colour where it means something.** The balance card shows the budget as a slim meter, the remaining share in brand green, with "left" and "spent" beneath instead of a sentence of figures. Each plan card has a faint brand light along its top edge, a little stronger for each tier, so the plans read as a progression without the page claiming one is "most popular"; the current plan glows most, gets a green-tinted edge and "Your plan". A card’s light brightens while its button is hovered or focused, not the whole card, since only the button acts; one hue throughout, because tiers are steps on one ladder and brightness says "more" better than colour. Check marks are brand green at 75%. Buttons stay neutral: green is expression, never a control. The plans sit three across only when the section is wide enough for each price on one line (a container query), and stack otherwise, never two and one. Premium steps up in the material of each card, not in a badge: the first plan is the plain card, the middle one a lifted surface (`--surface-lifted`) with a green-only glow at its top and an edge that starts green and fades, and the top one lit glass, an aurora field in brand green, teal and a trace of violet (`--aurora-teal`, `--aurora-violet`) frosted under blur, inside an edge that runs from green into teal, with a sheen that crosses it while its button is hovered (off with reduced motion). Tints scale by `--aurora-strength`, gentler on the light theme. On the light theme the fields and the top-edge glow are replaced by one even wash across the top of the card (green into teal on the top plan, green on the middle), fading down: on white, soft fields read as smudges. A surface lift alone didn't separate the first two plans; the middle plan needs a quieter share of the same light. The materials are Home's, so the top plan feels like the product at its fullest; they follow position, so they suit any operator's plans. Plans are told apart by what differs, not by icons: the allowance as a second headline with a bar against the largest plan, and a list of only what the plan adds ("Everything in Creator, plus:"). A small tier mark beside each name was tried as signal bars, stacked layers and concentric rings and dropped: it only restated rank, which the order, the light and the allowance already show. Repeats of the plan below are dropped by `planLadder`; providers give flat feature lists (engine question Q9). | The page had layout but no emphasis; colour now marks what's left, what's yours and what's more. | `PlanSettings`, `BudgetMeter`, `TierLight`, `Aurora`, `materialFor`, `Allowance`, `lib/plan-ladder.ts` | — |
